# Art direction v1 (replaces the flat-vector art bible)

Status: draft from the reference board of 16 images. Provisional where no references exist yet (the room, the Dashami Sindoor Khela moment). All Bengali strings are NEEDS-NATIVE-REVIEW.

## 1. The style call

The references fall into three families. We take something specific from each.

| Family | Reference images | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Painted dusk and night, haze and one warm light** | 9 and 12 (night street procession), 10 (night river), 11 (foggy dawn river, watercolour), 3 (dusk ghat) | **The main look.** Soft atmosphere, muted dark world, the idol as the single warm glow, crowds as silhouettes |
| **Flat layered figures with fireflies and smoke** | 13, 14, 15, 16 | **The technique.** Flat figures on separate layers over a textured world; fireflies, smoke ribbons and light dots as the moving layer. This is what makes parallax and animation easy |
| **Vintage print** | 5 (Mahishasuramardini poster) | **Lettering and paper.** Brush Bangla titles in alta red, crumpled paper, brown woodcut Durga print |
| **Documentary photography** | 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 | **Fact, not mood.** Clothes, boat shapes, idol proportions, the courtyard architecture, kaash and paddy colour |

**One-line style:** *painted, hazy, one warm light, flat layered figures, vintage-print Bangla lettering, film grain.*

## 2. Principles that come out of the board

1. **The idol is small and glowing, not a hero close-up.** In almost every strong reference the protima is a distant warm light in a muted world, surrounded by people. This also keeps the deity reverent and the painting workload sane.
2. **One light source per scene.** Everything else sits in blue, green, brown or fog.
3. **Scale and people.** Figures are small, in groups, in white dhotis and red-bordered saris, yellow shirts, red dhaak. People are painted as shapes; the painter invents faces. No real faces from the photos.
4. **Haze is a layer,** not a filter: fog, smoke, incense and dust are separate layers that move.
5. **Grain and paper over everything,** applied in code over the whole frame.

## 3. The three sets and their states

### Set 1: The room (Mahalaya, 4 AM) , PROVISIONAL, no references yet
- **Composition:** a Kolkata flat, interior, a window with wooden louvered shutters at one side, a shelf or table at the centre with a valve radio glowing, a doorway at the other side.
- **Light:** dark, the radio dial the only warm point, a faint blue pre-dawn at the window that lightens as the broadcast plays.
- **Props:** valve radio, brass thali with a lamp, shiuli flowers in a bowl, folded new clothes with tags, Pujo-sankhya magazines, wall calendar with a Durga picture, ceiling fan, tea cups, kaash flowers in a vase.
- **People:** a grandparent by the radio, a child half asleep. Painted as shapes.
- **Motion:** dial glow, fan, curtain, dust in the window light, window slowly brightening.
- **Hotspots:** radio dial (player), thali (lamp), door (go to Shashthi).

### Set 2: The Thakurdalan courtyard (Shashthi, Ashtami, Navami, Dashami morning)
Based on image 4. An old house courtyard: arched dalan with the protima glowing at the back, balconies with green louvres, marigold-garland arches, red hanging lantern covers, bird net across the sky, peacock-and-lotus pillar ornament, a stair of patterned tiles.
- **Shashthi, dusk:** blue hour sky, lights just switching on, the face just unveiled, a few early visitors.
- **Ashtami, morning then midnight:** bright morning with a Pushpanjali crowd in white; the midnight state is the Sandhi Pujo with 108 lamps.
- **Navami, night:** warm dark, dhunuchi dancers in the foreground, thick smoke.
- **Dashami, morning:** Sindoor Khela in red powder, crowd in white and red.
- **Hotspots:** thali (Pushpanjali, Aarti, Dhunuchi), the conch and the bell as tappable objects, the lamp tiers.
- A future edition can swap this for a para pandal. Each year gets a new set.

### Set 3: The river and the fields (Saptami dawn, Dashami dusk)
- **Saptami, dawn:** fog, kaash in the foreground, a boat, the Kola Bou snan, a pale peach sky (references 11 and 6 for mood).
- **Dashami, dusk:** amber light on dark water, the idol on boats among glowing lamps, crowd on the steps (references 10, 3 and 9).
- **Transition use:** processions through kaash and paddy (references 1, 2, 7, 8) are the "walking" transition between sets.
- **Hotspots:** the boat, the lamps in the water, the closing slate "আসছে বছর আবার".

## 4. Layer breakdown (what the painter delivers per scene)

Layered files (PSD or Procreate), 16:9, minimum 4000 x 2250 px, with the subject inside a central safe area, and the background painted **beyond** every foreground object so parallax never shows a hole.

1. sky
2. far background
3. mid background and architecture
4. idol and its glow (separate from everything)
5. mid figures (groups, each group separate)
6. foreground figures and objects
7. light layers (lamps, window light, glow), as separate layers, not baked in
8. fx plates we animate: smoke ribbons, fog banks, firefly dots, petals

We export WebP or AVIF per layer, and add in code: parallax, light flicker, smoke and fog drift, fireflies, grain, dial and lamp glow.

## 5. Lettering and paper

- Big Bangla title across each scene, in brush lettering in alta red or paper white, in the style of reference 5. The lettering is drawn by a lettering artist, not set in a font.
- Working titles: **মহালয়ার ভোর** for the room. Day names for the rest: **ষষ্ঠী, সপ্তমী, অষ্টমী, নবমী, দশমী**. Closing slate: **আসছে বছর আবার**.
- The UI sits on crumpled-paper and printed-label textures.

## 6. Rules that still stand

- No generative AI in any final art. Human-painted, with provenance logged.
- The protima: calm, glowing, never flickering or distorted.
- Reference images are for the painter's eyes. They are **not** shipped on the site.
